# Guide: The Negotiation Round

You're put in a live conflict with a customer or internal stakeholder over scope,
timeline, or expectations. Example: a customer demands extra features inside a
fixed 4-week pilot, or pushes back on a deadline you can't safely hit.

**What great looks like**
1. **Clarify first** — what is driving the ask (a launch date, an exec promise, a
   real user pain?), who decides, what is fixed vs. flexible, and what "success"
   means to them.
2. **Decompose the ask** — split it into must-have, nice-to-have, and later. Offer
   a scoped first deliverable that fits the constraint, with the rest sequenced.
3. **Name the riskiest assumption** — e.g. "the extra feature needs data we
   haven't seen" — and propose a way to de-risk it (a spike, a short pilot, a
   fallback) before committing.
4. **Communicate the trade-offs** — offer options, not a flat "no": "we can add X
   if we drop Y or move the date by Z." Say what you're *not* doing and why, in
   plain language, and confirm agreement in writing.

**Anti-patterns:** saying yes to everything to keep the peace; a flat "no" with
no alternative; arguing positions instead of underlying needs; hiding a slipping
date until the last minute.
